The Prisoner's Dilemma of NBA Superteams

Why NBA teams can rationally chase superstar-heavy rosters even when the collective result leaves teams with less flexibility, greater risk, and surprisingly little competitive advantage.

By S. Brunello

Imagine two NBA teams entering an offseason with championship aspirations. Each faces essentially the same decision: aggressively pursue another superstar, or trust the roster already in place.

If neither team makes the move, both may preserve depth, financial flexibility, and future assets. But if one team adds another star while the other stands still, the team that stayed conservative risks falling behind.

That creates a familiar economic problem.

A Basketball Prisoner's Dilemma

In game theory, a prisoner's dilemma occurs when individually rational decisions can produce a collectively worse outcome. NBA roster construction can sometimes create a similar incentive.

Teams do not build their rosters in isolation. Every major trade, signing, and superstar pairing changes the incentives facing competing teams.

Why Teams Keep Chasing Stars

The incentive is easy to understand. Elite players are scarce, championships are valuable, and front offices operate in an environment where standing still can itself be risky.

The interesting question is not simply whether superteams work. It is why teams may continue pursuing them even when doing so introduces substantial costs.

The Cost of Going All In

Superstar-heavy teams can sacrifice roster depth, draft capital, salary-cap flexibility, and resilience to injuries.

Those trade-offs become particularly important under modern NBA salary-cap rules, where expensive rosters face increasingly severe restrictions.
